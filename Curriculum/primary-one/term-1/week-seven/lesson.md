# Week 7 — My First Algorithm
**Primary 1 · Term 1 · Single Class: 60–70 minutes**

---

## Overview

Everything this term has been building to this moment. Students learn the word *algorithm* and write their first one — a precise, numbered set of instructions for a real task. They also navigate a simple grid using directional algorithm instructions, experiencing firsthand how a computer follows steps exactly.

---

## Purpose

To name and formalise the concept of an algorithm, connecting it to everything students have already done: sequences (Week 4), instructions (Week 3), and patterns (Weeks 5–6). Students leave knowing that an algorithm is not a scary technical word — it is something they have been writing all term.

---

## Objectives

- Say what an algorithm is in their own words
- Write a step-by-step algorithm for a simple, familiar task with at least 5 steps
- Understand that an algorithm must be precise enough for someone unfamiliar with the task to follow
- Navigate a simple grid using directional algorithm instructions

---

## Preparation

- Draw a 5×5 grid on the board; mark START and FINISH; place 2 obstacles (drawn Xs or chairs)
- Prepare one 4×4 grid sheet per pair for the paired navigation round — printed or hand-drawn
- Write FORWARD · BACK · TURN LEFT · TURN RIGHT on the board before class
- If using video: download or queue up the video
- Estimated prep time: 20 minutes

---

## Helpful Links

- 📺 YouTube Video: *"The Bug Busters!"* — animated story, Kofi and Adaeze, 6 min
- 📄 Activity Book Pages 19–21
- 🖨️ 4×4 grid template — one per pair

## Key Vocabulary

| Word | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Algorithm** | A set of clear, ordered steps that solves a problem |
| **Precise** | Exact and specific — no guessing needed |
| **Navigate** | Move from one place to another by following directions |
| **Action word** | A verb that starts an instruction: pour, walk, turn, put… |

---

## Teaching Guide

### 1. Bridge from Week 6 *(5 min)*

Students swap Page 18 booklets and complete their partner's unfinished pattern. Then bridge: *"You have followed instructions, sorted sequences, and spotted patterns. Today all three come together — and the name for that combination is an algorithm."*

Write **ALGORITHM** on the board. Ask the class to say it together three times.

---

### 2. Video — "The Bug Busters!" *(6 min)*

Play before any teacher explanation. Kofi and Adaeze try to guide a robot to the library — first with vague instructions (failure), then with a precise numbered algorithm (success).

Pause after the robot's first failure: *"What went wrong? What would you change?"* Take 3–4 answers before continuing.

> **No screen?** Tell the story live. Use two student volunteers as the robot — walk them through failed instructions first, then corrected precise instructions.

---

### 3. Game — Grid Navigation *(19 min)*

**Round 1 — Whole class (10 min):** A student volunteer is the robot on the floor grid (or at the board). The class gives one instruction at a time. Teacher follows them literally. How many steps to reach FINISH? Can they do it in fewer?

**Round 2 — Pairs (9 min):** One student draws a path on their 4×4 grid sheet. The other writes the algorithm without seeing the path (forward X, turn right, forward X…). They swap and follow the written instructions. Do they reach the same endpoint?

Only four words allowed: FORWARD · BACK · TURN LEFT · TURN RIGHT.

---

### 4. Activity Book — Pages 19 & 20 *(20 min)*

Page 19: students choose one familiar task and write a numbered algorithm of at least 5 steps. Each step must start with an action word and be specific enough that a robot could follow it without asking a question.

Page 20: a 4-panel comic strip showing a character following the Page 19 algorithm — one panel per step (or two steps per panel for longer algorithms).

Page 21 goes home: students write a 6-step algorithm for something they do at home, test it on a family member, and note where it worked or where the person got confused.

---

### 5. Share & Close *(10 min)*

Ask 3 pairs to read out their grid navigation algorithms. Class follows along on the board — does the robot reach FINISH?

Close: *"An algorithm is a clear, ordered set of instructions that solves a problem. You have been writing algorithms all term — today you learned their name."*

**Bridge:** *"Next week we look at what happens when an algorithm goes WRONG — and we learn the name for fixing it."*
