# Week 2 — Input & Output
**Primary 1 · Term 1 · Single Class: 60–70 minutes**

---

## Overview

Students discover that every computer interaction follows the same pattern: something goes in, something happens in the middle, and something comes back out. They experience this first through a whole-class guessing game, then through a video, body movement examples, and a group paper machine build.

---

## Purpose

To give students a mental model for how computers work — not as black boxes, but as predictable input/process/output systems. This model underpins everything that follows in the curriculum, from sequencing to debugging.

---

## Objectives

- Explain input and output in their own words
- Give at least 3 real-life examples of an input → output pair
- Understand that a process happens in the middle to turn input into output
- Complete an IPO diagram (Input → Process → Output) in their Activity Book

---

## Preparation

- Write INPUT, PROCESS, OUTPUT on the board before class — leave it up all lesson
- Fold one A4 sheet into thirds per group of 3 (for the paper machine build)
- Optional: decorate a cardboard box labelled "THE MACHINE" for the no-screen version of the video
- Estimated prep time: 15 minutes

---

## Helpful Links

- 📺 YouTube Video: *"In Goes This, Out Comes That!"* — presenter + decorated box, Nigerian examples, 5 min
- 📄 Activity Book Pages 4–6

---

## Key Vocabulary

| Word | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Input** | What you put into a computer |
| **Process** | What the computer does with it |
| **Output** | What the computer gives back |
| **IPO** | Short for Input → Process → Output |

---

## Teaching Guide

### 1. Bridge from Week 1 *(5 min)*

Ask 3–4 volunteers to share their Page 3 Computer Hunt findings from home. If children forgot it, ask verbally: *"Did anyone spot a computer at home? What was it doing?"*

---

### 2. Warm-Up — The Machine Game *(7 min)*

Teacher stands at the front: *"I am a machine. You give me something — I give you something back."* Secretly decide on a rule, then play:

- Number rule: *"I add 2."* Student says *"3,"* teacher says *"5."* Students guess the rule.
- Word rule: *"I say the opposite."* *"Hot"* → *"cold."* *"Big"* → *"small."*

After a few rounds, point to the three words on the board — INPUT, PROCESS, OUTPUT — and name what just happened in the game.

---

### 3. Video — "In Goes This, Out Comes That!" *(5 min)*

Play after the machine game. Students already have the vocabulary — the video reinforces it with Nigerian examples (ATM, school bell, phone calculator).

> **No screen?** Act out the same scenario using a decorated box. Put a number card in, pull out a card showing the result. Students take turns being the machine.

---

### 4. Discussion — Your Body as an I/O Machine *(8 min)*

*"Your body is also an input/output machine!"* Act out each example:

- Loud noise (input) → brain reacts (process) → you jump (output)
- Someone tells a joke (input) → brain processes → you laugh (output)

Students suggest their own. Write each on the board in three columns: INPUT | PROCESS | OUTPUT. Accept any description for the process column — even *"thinks"* or *"feels"* is right.

---

### 5. Group Activity — Build a Paper Machine *(20 min)*

Each group of 3 gets a folded A4 sheet and designs an imaginary machine — labelling the IN slot, the PROCESS box, and the OUT slot with drawings and words.

Encourage creative machines: a jollof machine, a homework machine, a happiness machine. The logic is the same regardless.

After building, groups share in 30 seconds each. The class guesses what went in and what came out before the group reveals it. Ask: *"What happens if you put in the wrong thing?"*

---

### 6. Activity Book — Pages 4 & 5 *(13 min)*

Students work through Pages 4 and 5 in class. Page 5 is the key assessment piece — collect it at the end of class.

Page 6 goes home as light reinforcement, or use it as a 5-minute starter at the beginning of Week 3.

---

### 7. Share & Close *(5 min)*

Ask 3 volunteers to read their Page 5 fill-in lines: *"The input is ___ and the output is ___."*

**Bridge:** *"We know a computer takes something in and gives something back. But WHO decides what happens in the middle? Those instructions have a special name. Next week we find out what it is."*
