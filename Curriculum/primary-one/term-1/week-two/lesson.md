# The Coding Clubhouse
## Primary 1 · Term 1 · Week 2 — Input & Output

**Single Class: 60–70 minutes · Activity Book: Pages 4–6 · 100% Offline-Capable**

> **GOAL:** Students understand that computers take something in, do something with it, and give something back

---

> ⚠️ **ONE CLASS ADAPTATION**
> Two 40-minute sessions have been merged into one 60–70 minute class. The physical machine game and video/story happen in the first 20 minutes. The paper machine group build, discussion, and Activity Book pages 4–5 happen in the remaining time. Page 6 goes home as optional reinforcement — or can be used as a 5-minute starter for Week 3.

---

> 🔗 **BRIDGE FROM WEEK 1**
> Start by asking 3–4 volunteers to share their Page 3 Computer Hunt findings from home. This closes the Week 1 take-home loop and surfaces real-world examples that feed directly into today's input/output discussion. If children forgot to bring it back, ask verbally: *"Did anyone spot a computer at home? What was it doing?"*

---

## Learning Objectives

- Explain input and output in their own words — "what goes in" and "what comes out"
- Give at least 3 real-life examples of an input → output pair
- Understand that the computer does something in the middle (process) to turn input into output
- Complete a 3-box IPO diagram (Input → Process → Output) in their Activity Book

## 🟠 WARM-UP: The Machine Game
**Whole class · 7 min**

Teacher stands at the front: *"I am a machine. You give me something — I give you something back."* Secretly decide on a rule.

- Start simple — *"I add 2 to any number."* A student says *"3,"* teacher says *"5."* Students guess the rule.
- Switch to a word rule: *"I say the opposite."* Student says *"hot,"* teacher says *"cold."* *"Big"* → *"small."*

**Introduce the three words by writing them on the board:**
- **INPUT** — what you give the machine
- **PROCESS** — what the machine does
- **OUTPUT** — what comes back

These stay on the board all week.

---

## 🔴 YOUTUBE VIDEO — "In Goes This, Out Comes That!"
**Watch in class · 5 min**

**When to play:** After the machine game and the 3 words are on the board. Students already have the vocabulary — they will recognise INPUT and OUTPUT and feel confident.

**What it covers:** A presenter uses a decorated cardboard box labelled "THE MACHINE." Children in the video put things in and the machine gives something back. Second half uses Nigerian examples — ATM, school bell, phone calculator. Encourage students to call out answers when the presenter pauses.

> **NO SCREEN ALTERNATIVE:** Teacher acts out the same scenario using a real decorated box. Put a number card in, pull out a card with the number +2. Same lesson, no technology needed.

---

## 🔵 DISCUSSION: Your Body as an I/O Machine
**Whole class · 8 min**

*"Your body is also an input/output machine!"* Act out each example physically:

- Eyes see food (input) → brain processes → mouth waters (output)
- Ears hear loud noise (input) → brain processes → you jump (output)
- Someone tells a joke (input) → brain processes → you laugh (output)

Students suggest their own. Write each on the board in 3 columns: **INPUT | PROCESS | OUTPUT**

When filling in PROCESS, ask: *"What does your brain do in between?"* Even rough answers like *"thinks"* or *"decides"* are perfect.

---

## 🔵 GROUP ACTIVITY: Build a Paper Machine
**Groups of 3 · 20 min**

Each group gets one sheet of A4 paper folded into thirds. They design an imaginary machine:

- **Left third — IN slot:** Draw what goes in (e.g. dirty clothes, sad feelings, raw yam)
- **Middle third — PROCESS box:** Draw what the machine does inside (e.g. washes, cheers up, cooks)
- **Right third — OUT slot:** Draw what comes out (e.g. clean clothes, a smile, pounded yam)

Encourage creative ideas — *a homework machine, a jollof machine, a happiness machine.*

After groups finish, ask: **"What happens if you put in the wrong thing?"** This plants the idea that the output always depends on the input.

Groups hold up their machines and share in 30 seconds each. The class guesses what went in and what came out before the group reveals it.

## 🟠 ACTIVITY BOOK — Page 4: My Body Machine
**In class · 8 min**

Students open to Page 4. Teacher reads the heading: *"Your body is an input/output machine!"*

**Page 4 layout:**
- Top section: Simple outline drawing of a child with 3 arrows — pointing IN (left), through the head (process), pointing OUT (right)
- Row 1 — pre-filled example: INPUT: *"loud noise"* → PROCESS: *"brain reacts"* → OUTPUT: *"you jump"*
- Rows 2 and 3 — blank: Three connected boxes with arrows. Children fill in their own pairs.

> *Note: The process box is always the hardest — accept any description of an action happening, including "thinks," "feels," or a drawing of a brain.*

---

## 🟠 ACTIVITY BOOK — Page 5: My IPO Diagram
**In class · 5 min**

Teacher draws the 3-box template on the board as a reference.

**Page 5 layout:**
- Instruction line: *"Draw or write something a computer does in the boxes below."*
- Main area: One large blank IPO diagram — big LEFT box labelled INPUT, arrow, MIDDLE box labelled PROCESS, arrow, RIGHT box labelled OUTPUT. Boxes large enough to draw inside.
- Below diagram: *"The input is: ___"* and *"The output is: ___"* — two fill-in lines
- Challenge star: *"Can you draw a SECOND example on the back of this page?"*

> *Collect Page 5 at the end of class. It forms part of the Week 2 assessment and goes into the student's term portfolio.*

---

## 🟠 ACTIVITY BOOK — Page 6: I/O Match
**TAKE-HOME / Early finishers**

Early finishers can begin Page 6 in class. Otherwise it goes home as light reinforcement.

**Page 6 layout:**
- Title: *"Match the input to the output!"*
- Left column (5 inputs): Press a key · Speak into microphone · Put money in ATM · Type 2+3 · Touch phone screen
- Right column (5 outputs, shuffled): Money comes out · Answer appears (5) · A letter appears · Voice is recorded · App opens

> *Tip: Page 6 doubles as a quick 5-minute starter for Week 3 — use it to check retention before introducing algorithms.*

---

## SHARE & CLOSE
**Whole class · 5 min**

Ask 3 volunteers to read their Page 5 fill-in lines: *"The input is ___ and the output is ___."* Celebrate each answer.

> **BRIDGE TEASER:** *"We now know a computer takes something in and gives something back. But WHO decides what happens in the middle? Let children guess. Those instructions have a special name. Next week we find out what it is."*

## 🩵 MATERIALS NEEDED

**Always available:**
- Crayons, pencils, Activity Book (one per student)
- Chalk or whiteboard marker

**Prep required:**
- One A4 sheet per group folded into thirds (paper machine build)
- Optional: decorated box labelled "THE MACHINE" for the no-screen version of the video
- Prep time: approx. 15 minutes — fold group sheets, read discussion questions once, pre-download video if available

**Optional (if device available):**
- Projector or TV screen to show the YouTube video

---

## 📝 TEACHER NOTES

- **Machine game before the video.** The game gives students the vocabulary in their bodies before the video names it on screen. Do not reverse the order.
- Leave **INPUT / PROCESS / OUTPUT** on the board all week. Students glance at it during activity book work.
- **Embrace the silly paper machines.** A "jollof machine" is just as valid as a washing machine. If a child describes a fantastical machine, ask them to label the input and output carefully — the logic still applies.
- **The "wrong input" question matters.** Asking groups *"what happens if you put the wrong thing in?"* plants the idea that computers only do exactly what they are told — preparation for debugging in Week 8.
- With one session, keep the paper machine build tightly timed at 20 minutes. Warn groups when 5 minutes remain.
- **Page 5 collection:** this is the key assessment artefact for Week 2. Ensure every student attempts it even briefly before the end of class.

---

## ✅ ASSESSMENT — How to Know If This Week Landed

- **Activity Book Page 4:** child has filled in at least 2 rows of body I/O examples — words or drawings accepted
- **Activity Book Page 5:** child's IPO diagram has something in all 3 boxes and the fill-in lines are attempted
- **Paper machine:** group's machine has a labelled IN slot, a described process, and a labelled OUT slot
- **Verbal:** child can answer *"what is an input?"* and *"what is an output?"* in their own words or with an example
- **Bridge check:** child can complete *"WHO decides what happens inside the computer?"* — any answer pointing toward *"us"* or *"people"* is a full pass
