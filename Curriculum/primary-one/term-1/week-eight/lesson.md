# Week 8 — Debugging: Fix the Mistake
**Primary 1 · Term 1 · Single Class: 60–70 minutes**

---

## Overview

Students learn the word *debugging* and discover that finding and fixing errors is a normal, essential part of coding — not a sign of failure. Through a bug-origin story, spot-the-bug card pairs, and a group buggy recipe game, they develop the habit of reading algorithms critically and fixing what is wrong.

---

## Purpose

To normalise mistakes and build the debugging mindset early. Students who learn at age 6–7 that bugs are expected — and fixable — carry that resilience into every year of coding that follows. This week also consolidates grid navigation from Week 7 by applying it in a debugging context.

---

## Objectives

- Use the word "bug" correctly to mean an error in an algorithm
- Identify a bug in a given set of instructions
- Fix a bug by changing, removing, or reordering the incorrect step
- Understand that debugging is a normal, essential part of coding

---

## Preparation

- Write or print 4 "Spot the Bug" algorithm cards per pair — each with one deliberate error (see Teaching Guide for suggested bugs)
- Write or print 1 "Buggy Recipe" sheet per group of 4 — with 2 hidden bugs
- Scrap paper or mini whiteboards for writing corrections
- Estimated prep time: 30 minutes (bug cards are the main prep item)

## Key Vocabulary

| Word | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Bug** | A mistake in an algorithm that makes it do the wrong thing |
| **Debug** | Find and fix a bug |
| **Trace** | Read through an algorithm step by step to find where it goes wrong |
| **Test** | Try out an algorithm to see if it works |

---

## Teaching Guide

### 1. Bridge from Week 7 + Bug Introduction *(10 min)*

Ask 3–4 volunteers to share their Page 21 take-home results. Focus on anyone who found a "got confused" step: *"Which step was the bug? How did you know?"*

Then tell the origin story: in the 1940s, a real moth got stuck inside an early computer. The engineer taped it into a notebook and wrote *"first actual case of a bug being found."* The name stuck.

Write on the board: **BUG** — a mistake in an algorithm. **DEBUGGING** — finding and fixing bugs.

Say it out loud: *"Every coder in the world debugs. It is not a sign you are bad at coding — it is a sign you are paying attention."*

---

### 2. Activity — Spot the Bug Cards *(15 min)*

Pairs take turns reading algorithm cards aloud — one step each. For each card, they identify the bug and write the corrected step.

Suggested bugs to prepare:
- Making garri: garri added before water is in the cup
- Morning routine: shoes put on before uniform
- Grid navigation: wrong turn that lands the robot in the wrong square
- Washing hands: hands rinsed before soap is added

---

### 3. Game — Buggy Recipe Group Game *(15 min)*

Each group of 4 gets a recipe algorithm (e.g. making a cup of Milo) with 2 hidden bugs. Groups race to find both bugs and write the corrected algorithm.

After all groups finish, compare answers. Where groups disagree, discuss: can more than one fix be valid? Then ask: *"How did you test your algorithm? How did you know the fix was right?"* Guide toward: trace through the steps one by one and check each one makes sense.

---

### 4. Activity Book — Pages 22 & 23 *(20 min)*

Page 22: 3 broken algorithms. Students circle each bug and rewrite the corrected step, then complete: *"The bug was: ___ . I fixed it by: ___."* Note: the final algorithm has no bugs — students must write "no bug found."

Page 23: a grid navigation problem where the robot followed an algorithm and ended up in the wrong square. Students trace the path, identify the step that caused the error, and rewrite the corrected algorithm.

Page 24 goes home: students return to their Page 19 algorithm from Week 7, improve it with any bugs fixed and vague steps made more precise, then test the new version on someone at home.

---

### 5. Share & Close *(10 min)*

Ask: *"What was the hardest bug to find today? Why?"* Take 3–4 responses.

Close: *"Debugging is not the opposite of coding — it IS coding. The best coders in the world spend more time debugging than writing new code. You are already doing what real coders do."*

**Bridge:** *"Next week is our final week of Term 1. You will put everything together — sequences, patterns, algorithms, and debugging — into one illustrated Algorithm Book to show your family."*
