# Week 4 — Sequences
**Primary 1 · Term 1 · Single Class: 60–70 minutes**

---

## Overview

Students formalise the idea of sequences — steps that must happen in a specific, fixed order. Through a morning routine card sort, a relay race, and their own written sequences, they experience what happens when steps are swapped and why order is not optional.

---

## Purpose

To give students a precise word and concept for something they have been doing since Week 3: ordering steps. The shift from "instructions" to "sequence" prepares them for algorithms — where order is everything.

---

## Objectives

- Define a sequence as a set of steps that must happen in a specific order
- Correctly order at least 5 steps for a familiar everyday routine
- Explain why changing the order of one step causes a problem
- Create their own 5-step sequence for an action of their choice

---

## Preparation

- Prepare morning routine card sets — 6–8 illustrated cards per group showing a scrambled morning routine (wake up, brush teeth, dress, eat, pack bag, leave)
- Prepare 5 large numbered wall cards for the relay race — in wrong order, one familiar sequence
- Tape to fix wall cards
- Estimated prep time: 25 minutes

## Key Vocabulary

| Word | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Sequence** | A set of steps in a specific, correct order |
| **Order** | The position of each step — 1st, 2nd, 3rd… |
| **Step** | One action in a sequence |
| **Result** | What happens when the sequence is followed |

---

## Teaching Guide

### 1. Bridge from Week 3 *(5 min)*

Ask 3–4 volunteers to share their Page 9 instruction sets from home. Ask the class to rate each: clear or not clear? This naturally raises the question of order — the entry point for today.

---

### 2. Activity — Morning Routine Card Sort *(15 min)*

Give each group a set of scrambled morning routine cards. Groups arrange the cards in the correct order and number each card 1–8.

After sorting, compare with another group. Discuss: which steps are flexible (pack bag can come before or after breakfast) and which are not (you cannot brush teeth before waking up)?

Write **SEQUENCE** on the board: a set of steps in a specific, correct order.

---

### 3. Game — Sequencing Relay Race *(10 min)*

Tape 5 large cards to the wall in the wrong order. Divide the class into two teams. One student per turn runs up and swaps two cards to improve the sequence. First team to get all 5 in the right order wins.

Use a sequence every student knows — washing hands or the school bell morning routine.

---

### 4. Activity Book — Pages 10 & 11 *(20 min)*

Page 10: students number 5 scrambled illustrated panels in the correct order and write one sentence explaining why their Step 1 must come first.

Page 11: students choose any familiar action and write or draw their own 5-step sequence, then complete: *"If I changed step ___ and step ___, the result would be: ___"*

Page 12 goes home: students create a sequence for something they do at home and bring it back to Week 5.

---

### 5. Share & Close *(10 min)*

Ask 3 volunteers to read out their Page 11 sequences while classmates act them out as robots. After each: *"Could we swap any steps? Would the result change?"*

**Bridge:** *"A sequence is not just a list — it is a list in the right order. Next week we look at something that hides inside sequences: patterns."*
