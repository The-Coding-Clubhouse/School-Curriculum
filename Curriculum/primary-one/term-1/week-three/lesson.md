# The Coding Clubhouse
## Primary 1 · Term 1 · Week 3 — Giving Instructions

**Single Class: 60–70 minutes · Activity Book: Pages 7–9 · 100% Offline-Capable**

> **GOAL:** Students give a clear set of step-by-step instructions and understand why order matters

---

> 🔗 **BRIDGE FROM WEEK 2**
> Use Page 6 (the I/O match from Week 2) as a 5-minute starter if children brought it back. If not, do a quick call-and-respond: *"What goes IN?"* (Input!) *"What comes OUT?"* (Output!) *"What happens in the MIDDLE?"* (Process!) Then reveal the bridge: *"The PROCESS is the instructions. Today we learn what instructions look like."*

---

## Learning Objectives

- Understand that instructions must be given in the right order to work
- Give a set of at least 4 clear, numbered steps for a simple everyday action
- Identify what goes wrong when instructions are in the wrong order (debugging instinct)
- Complete a 4-step comic strip in the Activity Book showing a sequence of instructions

## 🟠 WARM-UP: The Robot Teacher Game
**Whole class · 10 min**

Teacher announces: *"I am Robot Teacher. I only do exactly what I am told — nothing more, nothing less."*

Ask a student to give you instructions to *"pick up the chalk."* Follow instructions LITERALLY:

- If they say *"go to the board"* — walk all the way to the board but stop there.
- If they say *"pick up the chalk"* without saying *"reach down,"* just stand still with arms at sides.
- Make funny mistakes when instructions are unclear or out of order — class loves the absurdity.

After 3–4 attempts (getting progressively better), ask: **"Why did Robot Teacher keep getting it wrong?"** Land the answer: the instructions were not clear or not in the right order.

Write on the board: **INSTRUCTIONS MUST BE: 1. CLEAR  2. IN THE RIGHT ORDER**

---

## 🔴 YOUTUBE VIDEO — "Robot Amaka Goes Wrong!"
**Watch in class · 5 min**

**When to play:** After the Robot Teacher game, before the sequence card activity. Students already feel the frustration of unclear instructions — the video validates and extends that experience.

**What it covers:** A short comedy animation where a child named Amaka gives a robot instructions to make breakfast. Each wrong or vague instruction causes a funny disaster. By the end, Amaka has learned to number her steps and be specific. Ends with: *"Good instructions = happy robot. Bad instructions = pounded yam on the ceiling."*

**Pause moment:** Pause after Amaka's second mistake and ask the class: *"What did she do wrong?"* Let 3–4 children answer before continuing.

> **NO SCREEN ALTERNATIVE:** Teacher narrates the same Amaka story live, acting out the robot's mistakes dramatically. Have a student volunteer be "the robot" and follow bad instructions literally.

## 🔵 ACTIVITY: Sequence Picture Cards
**Pairs · 15 min**

Distribute a set of 4–5 picture cards per pair showing a familiar action in scrambled order. Suggested sequences (choose one per pair):

- **Making garri:** put water in cup → add garri → stir → it thickens → eat
- **Getting ready for school:** wake up → brush teeth → put on uniform → eat breakfast → leave
- **Washing hands:** go to tap → wet hands → add soap → rub → rinse → dry

Pairs arrange the cards in the correct order, then number them 1, 2, 3… on the back.

After pairs finish: ask one pair to read their steps aloud while another pair acts them out as a robot. Does the robot end up in the right place?

Ask: **"What would happen if we did step 3 before step 1?"** One pair demonstrates the wrong order — class identifies the problem.

## 🟠 ACTIVITY BOOK — Page 7: My 4-Step Comic Strip
**In class · 12 min**

Students open to Page 7. Teacher reads the heading: *"Draw the instructions!"*

**Page 7 layout:**
- Top: 4 large equal boxes arranged in a row, each numbered 1–4
- Below each box: a fill-in line labelled *"Step _: ___"*
- Bottom: *"My instructions are for: ___"* (fill in the action)
- Star prompt: *"Could a robot follow your instructions? Circle: YES / NOT YET"*

Students choose any familiar action (making tea, feeding a pet, brushing teeth, fetching water) and fill in 4 comic strip boxes — drawing the action in each box and writing or dictating the step below.

> *Note: Children who cannot yet write the steps can draw arrows between boxes and dictate — teacher or a helper writes the words.*

---

## 🟠 ACTIVITY BOOK — Page 8: Fix the Instructions!
**In class · 8 min**

**Page 8 layout:**
- Title: *"These instructions are broken. Put them in the right order!"*
- Main area: 5 instruction sentences for a familiar action (e.g. washing your uniform), numbered randomly and scrambled
- Students write the correct order in the boxes provided: 1st, 2nd, 3rd, 4th, 5th
- Bonus: *"Circle the step that would cause a disaster if you did it first."*

**Suggested scrambled sequence (uniform washing):**

| Label | Step |
|-------|------|
| C | Rub soap on the uniform |
| A | Fill the bucket with water |
| E | Hang the uniform to dry |
| B | Put the uniform in the water |
| D | Rinse out all the soap |

> *Correct order: A → B → C → D → E. Disaster if done first: C (rubbing soap without water first).*

---

## 🟠 ACTIVITY BOOK — Page 9: Write Your Own Instructions
**TAKE-HOME**

Teacher introduces Page 9 as homework to bring back next week.

**Page 9 layout:**
- Title: *"Be the teacher! Write 5 instructions for someone at home."*
- 5 numbered blank lines with space for drawing a small picture next to each
- Bottom: *"I taught someone to: ___"*
- Star challenge: *"Ask someone at home to follow your instructions. Did it work? Draw a tick or a cross."*

> *Students bring Page 9 back to Week 4. Teacher uses it as the bridging starter — 3–4 volunteers share their instructions and the class rates them: clear or not clear?*

---

## SHARE & CLOSE
**Whole class · 10 min**

Ask 3–4 volunteers to read their Page 7 comic strip steps aloud while the class acts them out as silent robots.

Debrief: **"What made some instructions easier to follow than others?"** Collect answers on the board. Aim for: *specific, numbered, in the right order.*

> **BRIDGE TEASER:** *"Next week we are going to look at sequences — that means putting steps in exactly the right order every time. Same idea, but now we give it a proper name."*

Send home Page 9 with clear instructions: *"Pick something you do every day. Write 5 steps. Ask someone at home to try following your instructions!"*

## 🩵 MATERIALS NEEDED

**Always available:**
- Crayons, pencils, Activity Book (one per student)
- Chalk or whiteboard marker

**Prep required:**
- Sequence picture card sets (4–5 cards per pair) — printed or hand-drawn. One sequence per pair, enough for the class.
- Prep time: approx. 25 minutes — draw or print card sets, read the robot teacher script once, choose video or prepare live story

**Optional (if device available):**
- Projector or TV screen to show the YouTube video

## 📝 TEACHER NOTES

- **The Robot Teacher game is the heart of this lesson.** Commit to it — the funnier the mistakes, the better children remember why order matters. Don't rush past it.
- Common child mistake: writing *"do it"* or *"make it"* without specifics. Ask: *"Could a robot who has never done this before understand that step?"* This question alone fixes 80% of vague instructions.
- **For Page 8,** read the scrambled steps aloud before students write — this helps children who are slower readers not fall behind on the sequencing task.
- If children cannot agree on the right order for the sequence cards, let both groups present their version and have the class vote. The disagreement is the lesson.
- **Do NOT introduce the word "algorithm" yet** — but you are building the intuition for it. The bridge comes in Week 7.
- Prep reminder: sequence card sets are the main prep item. If pressed for time, use the "getting ready for school" sequence — every child knows it and requires minimal explanation.

---

## ✅ ASSESSMENT — How to Know If This Week Landed

- **Activity Book Page 7:** child's comic strip has 4 boxes with drawings and at least 1 written or dictated step label — the sequence should make logical sense
- **Activity Book Page 8:** child has correctly reordered at least 4 of the 5 steps
- **Verbal:** child can answer *"what happens if you put step 3 before step 1?"* with any variation of *"it goes wrong"* or *"it doesn't work"*
- **Sequence card sort:** pair has the cards in the correct order and can explain one step to the class
- **Attitude marker:** child spontaneously notices an out-of-order sequence somewhere else (in a story, in daily life) — this is the thinking transferring
