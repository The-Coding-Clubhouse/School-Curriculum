# Week 3 — Giving Instructions
**Primary 1 · Term 1 · Single Class: 60–70 minutes**

---

## Overview

Students discover that instructions only work when they are clear and in the right order. Through the Robot Teacher game, a comedy video, and a sequence card activity, they experience firsthand what happens when instructions are vague or scrambled — and how to fix them.

---

## Purpose

To build the intuition that underlies all programming: a machine does exactly what it is told, so the instructions must be precise and correctly ordered. Students leave with a felt sense of this, not just a definition.

---

## Objectives

- Understand that instructions must be clear and in the right order
- Give a set of at least 4 numbered steps for a simple everyday action
- Identify what goes wrong when instructions are out of order
- Complete a 4-step comic strip showing a sequence of instructions

---

## Preparation

- Prepare sequence picture card sets — 4–5 cards per pair, one familiar sequence per pair (suggestions below). Hand-drawn on card paper is fine.
- Suggested sequences: making garri, getting ready for school, washing hands
- Write on the board before class: **INSTRUCTIONS MUST BE: 1. CLEAR  2. IN THE RIGHT ORDER**
- If using video: download or queue up the video
- Estimated prep time: 25 minutes (card sets are the main prep item)

## Key Vocabulary

| Word | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Instruction** | A step that tells something what to do |
| **Sequence** | Steps in a specific, correct order |
| **Clear** | Easy to understand with no guessing needed |
| **Order** | The position of each step — 1st, 2nd, 3rd… |

---

## Teaching Guide

### 1. Bridge from Week 2 *(5 min)*

Use Page 6 (I/O match) as a quick starter if children brought it back. If not, do a call-and-respond: *"What goes IN?"* (Input!) *"What comes OUT?"* (Output!) *"What happens in the MIDDLE?"* (Process!)

Then bridge: *"The PROCESS is the instructions. Today we learn what instructions look like."*

---

### 2. Warm-Up — The Robot Teacher Game *(10 min)*

Announce: *"I am Robot Teacher. I only do exactly what I am told — nothing more, nothing less."*

Ask a student to give you instructions to pick up the chalk. Follow them literally — if they say *"go to the board"* without saying *"pick up the chalk,"* just stand there. Make the mistakes funny. After 3–4 attempts (getting progressively better), ask: *"Why did Robot Teacher keep getting it wrong?"*

Land the answer: the instructions were not clear or not in the right order. Point to the board.

---

### 3. Video — "Robot Amaka Goes Wrong!" *(5 min)*

Play after the game. Students already feel the frustration of unclear instructions — the video validates it.

Pause after Amaka's second mistake and ask: *"What did she do wrong?"* Let 3–4 children answer before continuing.

> **No screen?** Tell the Amaka story live. Have a student volunteer be the robot and follow your deliberately bad instructions. Same lesson, same laughs.

---

### 4. Activity — Sequence Picture Cards *(15 min)*

Distribute one scrambled card set per pair. Students arrange the cards in the correct order and number them on the back.

After pairs finish, ask one pair to read their steps aloud while another pair acts them out as a robot. Then ask: *"What would happen if we did step 3 before step 1?"* Have a pair demonstrate the wrong order — the class identifies the problem.

---

### 5. Activity Book — Pages 7 & 8 *(20 min)*

Students complete Pages 7 and 8 in class. Page 7 is the comic strip — students choose any familiar action and draw 4 steps. Page 8 is a scrambled sequence to reorder.

Page 9 goes home: students write 5 instructions for something they do every day and test them on someone at home. They bring it back to Week 4.

---

### 6. Share & Close *(10 min)*

Ask 3–4 volunteers to read their Page 7 comic strip steps aloud while the class acts them out as silent robots.

Debrief: *"What made some instructions easier to follow than others?"* Collect answers on the board — aim for: specific, numbered, in the right order.

**Bridge:** *"Next week we give this idea a proper name — sequences. Same thing you did today, but now it has a title."*
