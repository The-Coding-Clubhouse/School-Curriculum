# The Coding Clubhouse
## Primary 1 · Term 1 · Week 1 — What Is a Computer?

**Single Class: 60–70 minutes · Activity Book: Pages 1–3 · 100% Offline-Capable**

> **GOAL:** Students name 3 things a computer does and identify one they have seen in real life

---

> ⚠️ **ONE CLASS ADAPTATION**
> This lesson has been adapted from two 40-minute sessions into one 60–70 minute class. All writing, drawing, and worksheet work is completed directly in the Activity Book — partly in class and partly as structured take-home. The physical warm-up, discussion, and video/storytelling happen together in the first half; students begin their Activity Book work in the second half and finish any remaining pages at home.

---

## Learning Objectives

- Say what a computer is in their own words
- Name at least 3 things a computer can do: calculate, store, communicate
- Understand that computers need instructions from people to work
- Identify a computer they have seen — phone, ATM, school computer, TV

---

## Class Flow at a Glance

| 0–5 min | 5–12 min | 12–25 min | 25–50 min | 50–60 min |
|---------|----------|-----------|-----------|-----------|
| Warm-up game | Video / Story | Discussion + Card Match | Activity Book p.1–2 | Share & close · Bridge to p.3 |

---

## 🟠 WARM-UP: Have You Seen a Computer?
**Whole class · 5 min**

Teacher asks the class to stand up, then works through three questions one at a time:

- **"Stand up if you have ever SEEN a computer."** — look around, celebrate who is standing.
- **"Sit down if you have ever TOUCHED one."** — fewer remain standing.
- **"Stay standing if you have one at HOME."** — acknowledge both groups warmly.

No wrong answers. The goal is to surface prior knowledge and get energy in the room before the video.

---

## 🔴 YOUTUBE VIDEO — "Computers Are Like Brains!"
**Watch in class · 5 min**

**When to play:** Immediately after the warm-up, before any teacher explanation. Let the video do the first teaching.

**What it covers:** An animated cartoon brain inside a friendly computer explains 3 things it does — calculate, store, communicate. Uses Nigerian everyday examples: the ATM near the market, Mum's phone that sends money, the school's electric bell. Ends with: *"And YOU can give the brain its instructions — that's called coding!"*

> **NO SCREEN ALTERNATIVE:** Teacher narrates the same story as a told tale — the cartoon brain, the ATM, the school bell — then proceeds with the same discussion questions below.

---

## 🔵 CLASS DISCUSSION: What Does a Computer Do?
**Whole class · 13 min**

After the video, ask three anchor questions:

- **"What were the 3 things the computer brain could do?"** — guide them to: calculate, store, communicate.
- **"Which one did you see in your own life?"** — connect to the video's Nigerian examples.
- **"Why does the computer need US to give it instructions?"** — land the key message: a computer does nothing on its own.

Write the 3 things on the board as students say them. These stay up for the rest of the lesson.

### Card Match Game — Pairs · 8 min

Distribute 8 printed or hand-drawn card pairs. Students in pairs match each device to its function:

| Device | Function |
|--------|----------|
| ATM machine | counts and gives money |
| Phone | sends messages and photos |
| Radio | plays sounds and music |
| Traffic light | follows a timer to change colours |
| Calculator | adds numbers |
| TV | shows pictures and video |
| School bell timer | rings at the right time |
| Laptop | stores work and connects to others |

After matching, ask: **"Which of these is a real computer?"** Guide them: a computer is programmable — you can change what it does. A fan only spins and you cannot give it new instructions.

---

## 🟠 ACTIVITY BOOK — Page 1: Draw Your Computer
**In class · 10 min**

Students open to Page 1. Teacher reads the heading aloud.

**Page 1 layout:**
- Top half: Large drawing box with prompt — *"Draw a computer you have seen or imagined."*
- Bottom half: Three fill-in boxes — *"My computer can _________"* (×3)
- Star prompt: *"I have seen this computer at: (circle one) home / school / market / hospital / somewhere else"*

> *Note: Children who cannot yet write can draw a symbol in each box. Teacher writes the word beside it during circulation.*

---

## 🟠 ACTIVITY BOOK — Page 2: Circle the Computers!
**In class · 8 min**

6 simple illustrations on the page — phone, fan, ATM, candle, laptop, bicycle. Students circle the computers and put an X on the rest.

**Page 2 layout:**
- Main area: 6 illustrations to circle or cross
- Below: *"I circled ___ computers."* (fill in the number)
- Bonus box: *"Draw one more computer you know that isn't on this page."*

> *Correct answers: phone, ATM, laptop (3 computers). If a child circles the fan, ask: "Can you give the fan new instructions to do something different?" rather than correcting directly.*

---

## 🟠 ACTIVITY BOOK — Page 3: My Computer Hunt
**TAKE-HOME activity**

Teacher introduces Page 3 as homework to bring back next week.

**Page 3 layout:**
- 3 rows, each with: blank drawing box + *"I found a computer at: ___"* + *"It can: ___"*
- Bottom: *"I found ___ computers in total."* with 5 stars to colour in

> *Students bring Page 3 back to Week 2. Teacher starts Week 2 by asking 3–4 children to share what they found — this bridges the two weeks naturally.*

---

## SHARE & CLOSE
**Whole class · 10 min**

Ask 3–4 volunteers to hold up their Page 1 drawings and say one thing their computer can do. Celebrate every answer.

Close with the class repeating together: **"A computer follows instructions. And WE write the instructions."**

> **BRIDGE TEASER:** *"Next week we are going to find out what happens when you put something INTO a computer and what comes back OUT. Come ready to be a machine!"*

---

## 🩵 MATERIALS NEEDED

**Always available:**
- Crayons, pencils, Activity Book (one per student)
- Chalk or whiteboard marker for board

**Prep required:**
- 8 device card pairs per group (printed or hand-drawn on card paper)
- Prep time: approx. 20 minutes — cut card pairs, read discussion questions once, download video if available

**Optional (if device available):**
- Projector or TV screen to show the YouTube video

---

## 📝 TEACHER NOTES

- **Video (or story) first, discussion second.** Resist explaining everything before the video. Let the video introduce the ideas — your discussion lands them. The sequence matters.
- If a child says *"a computer is a phone"* — they are right. Affirm and expand: *"Yes! A smartphone is a type of computer."*
- **The fan debate is productive.** Some children argue a fan is a computer because *"you press a button."* Guide them: you can't give a fan new instructions. It only has one behaviour.
- **Do NOT introduce the word "algorithm" yet.** This week is purely about what computers are.
- With one session, keep activity book time brisk. Students complete Pages 1 and 2 in class. Page 3 goes home. Move on confidently even if some children haven't finished Page 2 — the bonus box is optional.
- Circulate during activity book time. Write words beside drawings for students who cannot yet write — this keeps them from getting stuck.

---

## ✅ ASSESSMENT — How to Know If This Week Landed

- **Activity Book Page 1:** child has drawn something they call a computer and filled at least 1 labelling box — words or symbols accepted
- **Activity Book Page 2:** child correctly circles at least 2 of the 3 computers (phone, ATM, laptop)
- **Verbal:** child can answer *"What does a computer need from us?"* with any variation of *"instructions"*
- **Attitude marker:** child asks any question about computers during or after the lesson — curiosity is the real measure at this age
- **Take-home check:** review Page 3 at the start of Week 2. At least 1 computer found counts as a full pass
