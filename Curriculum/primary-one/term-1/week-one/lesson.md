# Week 1 — What Is a Computer?
**Primary 1 · Term 1 · Single Class: 60–70 minutes**

---

## Overview

Students are introduced to computers for the first time. Through a stand-up warm-up, an animated video (or told story), and a card matching game, they discover that computers are programmable machines that calculate, store, and communicate — and that they need instructions from people to do any of it.

---

## Purpose

To establish a shared, accurate understanding of what a computer is before any coding concept is introduced. Students leave knowing that a computer is not magic — it does exactly what it is told, nothing more.

---

## Objectives

- Say what a computer is in their own words
- Name at least 3 things a computer can do: calculate, store, communicate
- Understand that computers need instructions from people to work
- Identify a computer they have seen in real life — phone, ATM, school computer, TV

---

## Preparation

- Draw or print 8 device card pairs per group — each card shows a device on one side and its function on the other: ATM, phone, radio, traffic light, calculator, TV, school bell, laptop
- Write the three vocabulary words on the board before students arrive
- If using video: download or queue up the video and test the screen
- If no screen: read through the no-screen story version once so you can tell it naturally
- Estimated prep time: 20 minutes

## Key Vocabulary

| Word | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Computer** | A programmable machine that follows instructions |
| **Calculate** | Work out a number or solve a problem |
| **Store** | Save information to use later |
| **Communicate** | Send or receive a message |
| **Instruction** | A step that tells something what to do |

---

## Teaching Guide

### 1. Warm-Up — Have You Seen a Computer? *(5 min)*

Ask the whole class to stand, then work through three questions one at a time — students sit or stay standing based on their answer:

1. *"Stand up if you have ever SEEN a computer."*
2. *"Sit down if you have never TOUCHED one."*
3. *"Stay standing if you have one at HOME."*

No wrong answers. The goal is to surface prior knowledge before teaching anything.

---

### 2. Video — "Computers Are Like Brains!" *(5 min)*

Play the video immediately after the warm-up, before any teacher explanation.

> **No screen?** Tell the same story live: a cartoon brain inside a computer can calculate, store, and communicate. Use the ATM, a phone sending money, and the school bell as your examples. End with: *"And YOU can give the brain its instructions — that's called coding!"*

---

### 3. Discussion — What Does a Computer Do? *(13 min)*

Ask three questions to the whole class:

1. *"What were the 3 things the computer brain could do?"* → guide toward: calculate, store, communicate
2. *"Which one did you see in your own life?"*
3. *"Why does the computer need US to give it instructions?"* → a computer does nothing on its own

Write the 3 things on the board as students name them. Leave them up for the rest of the lesson.

**Card Match Game — pairs, 8 min.** Each pair matches device cards to function cards. After matching, ask: *"Which of these is a real computer?"* Guide them: a computer is programmable — you can change what it does. A fan only spins.

---

### 4. Activity Book — Pages 1 & 2 *(18 min)*

Students work through Pages 1 and 2 in class. Circulate and write words beside drawings for students who cannot yet write — keep them moving, not stuck.

Introduce Page 3 before students pack up: they find and draw computers they spot at home or at the market and bring it back next week.

---

### 5. Share & Close *(10 min)*

Ask 3–4 volunteers to hold up their Page 1 drawings and say one thing their computer can do.

Close with the whole class repeating: *"A computer follows instructions. And WE write the instructions."*

**Bridge:** *"Next week we find out what happens when you put something INTO a computer and what comes OUT. Come ready to be a machine!"*
