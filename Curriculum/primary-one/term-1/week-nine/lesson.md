# Week 9 — Term Project: My Algorithm Book
**Primary 1 · Term 1 · Single Class: 60–70 minutes**

---

## Overview

The final week of Term 1 is a celebration. Students assemble a 4-page illustrated Algorithm Book drawing on their best work from across the term, present one page to the class, and receive their Coder Badge certificate. No new content is introduced — this week is entirely about consolidation, showcase, and reflection.

---

## Purpose

To give students a tangible, shareable artefact that represents everything they have learned this term — and to end the term with confidence, pride, and a positive association with coding. The Algorithm Book becomes a bridge to parents and families, making the term's learning visible beyond the classroom.

---

## Objectives

- Assemble a coherent 4-page illustrated booklet demonstrating term learning
- Present one page to the class in their own words
- Give one specific, positive piece of feedback to a classmate
- Reflect on what was easy, hard, and interesting this term

---

## Preparation

- Print and write student names on Coder Badge certificates — one per student
- Prepare award stickers in 5 categories (or draw stars on sticky notes): Best Algorithm, Most Creative Pattern, Bug Buster, Big Thinker, Class Coder
- Staple or fold Activity Book pages 25–28 into booklet form
- Set out coloured pencils and crayons for final decoration
- Estimated prep time: 30 minutes

## Key Vocabulary

*(Review — no new vocabulary this week)*

| Word | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Algorithm** | A set of clear, ordered steps that solves a problem |
| **Sequence** | Steps in a specific, correct order |
| **Pattern** | Something that repeats in a predictable way |
| **Debug** | Find and fix a bug |

---

## Teaching Guide

### 1. Opening Video + Bridge *(10 min)*

Play *"I Am a Coder!"* at the very start, before any booklet work begins. Then ask 2–3 volunteers to share their Page 24 (debugged algorithm) — old version vs new version. What changed?

> **No screen?** Lead the Coder Pledge as a call-and-response before students begin: *"I am a coder. I solve problems. I make mistakes and fix them. I help others learn. I keep going."*

---

### 2. Booklet Assembly *(15 min)*

Students compile their Algorithm Book from Activity Book pages 25–28:

- **Page 25** — What Is a Computer? (refined drawing from Week 1)
- **Page 26** — My Sequence (best sequence from the term)
- **Page 27** — My Pattern (best pattern, with core labelled)
- **Page 28** — My Algorithm (the debugged best version from Page 24)

Students add a title page with their name and class, then decorate. Circulate to help with titles and spellings. Every student needs something on Page 28 — prioritise this if time is short.

---

### 3. Presentations *(30 min)*

Every student presents **one page** from their Algorithm Book:
1. Stand up and hold the page for the class to see
2. Say what the page is about in one sentence
3. Point to one thing and explain it

After each presentation, the class gives one clap and one compliment. Teacher models the first compliment to set the standard.

> **Large classes (30+):** run simultaneous pair-sharing for 15 minutes, then invite 5–6 to present to the full class. Every student still presents — just not all to the full group.

---

### 4. Awards & Certificates *(10 min)*

Hand out Coder Badge certificates with each student's name. Add one category sticker to each student's Algorithm Book:

- ⭐ Best Algorithm
- 🎨 Most Creative Pattern
- 🔍 Bug Buster
- 💡 Big Thinker
- 🌟 Class Coder

Every student receives a certificate. Create additional categories if needed — no student goes without.

---

### 5. Reflection & Close *(10 min)*

Three reflection questions — students answer with thumbs up / flat / down, then 2–3 verbal responses each:

- *"What was the hardest thing you learned this term?"*
- *"What was your favourite activity?"*
- *"What do you want to learn in Term 2?"*

Close with the Coder Pledge together, then:

**Term 2 teaser:** *"Next term we learn about decisions — IF this happens, THEN do that. You already know how to give instructions. Now we are going to make those instructions smart."*
