# Week 6 — Patterns: Create Your Own
**Primary 1 · Term 1 · Single Class: 60–70 minutes**

---

## Overview

Students move from recognising patterns to designing them. Using eraser stamping (or crayon drawing), a peer challenge game, and a gallery walk, they create and test original repeating patterns — and practise spotting errors in each other's work.

---

## Purpose

To consolidate pattern thinking by flipping the task: instead of extending a given pattern, students design from scratch. Creation requires a deeper understanding of the core unit than recognition alone. The peer challenge also introduces light debugging — finding errors in someone else's pattern.

---

## Objectives

- Design an original AB, AAB, or ABC repeating pattern
- Describe their pattern's core in words
- Extend a partner's pattern correctly
- Identify whether a pattern is correct or broken when shown by a peer

---

## Preparation

- Cut A4 sheets into thirds lengthwise — one strip per student
- Set up ink pads or diluted poster paint in 2–3 colours per table — OR set out crayons only
- Pencils with erasers for stamping (or students use fingertips)
- Estimated prep time: 20 minutes

## Key Vocabulary

| Word | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Core** | The repeating unit in a pattern |
| **AB / AAB / ABC** | Pattern types named by how many different items are in the core |
| **Extend** | Add more repetitions to an existing pattern |
| **Error** | A mistake that breaks the pattern's rule |

---

## Teaching Guide

### 1. Bridge from Week 5 *(5 min)*

Ask 3–4 volunteers to share their Page 15 Pattern Hunt findings. Class names the core of each one together.

---

### 2. Activity — Eraser Stamp Printing *(15 min)*

Each student stamps a repeating pattern across a paper strip using a pencil eraser dipped in paint, or draws one with crayons. Rules:
- Must have a clear core of at least 2 units
- Must repeat the core at least 4 times
- Must be able to say what their core is

When done, students write their core below the strip.

---

### 3. Game — Peer Pattern Challenge *(15 min)*

Pairs work through three rounds:

- **Round 1 — Extend it:** Student A hides the last 3 items of their strip. Student B predicts and draws them. Reveal and check.
- **Round 2 — Break it:** Student A changes one item deliberately. Student B finds the mistake and says what it should be.
- **Round 3 — Beat it:** Each student creates the most complex pattern they can and challenges their partner to name the core in under 10 seconds.

---

### 4. Activity Book — Pages 16 & 17 *(15 min)*

Page 16: students design 3 original patterns — each with a labelled core, at least 4 repetitions, and a pattern type name (AB, AAB, or ABC).

Page 17: 4 broken patterns on the page. Students circle the mistake in each and redraw the correct item.

Page 18 goes home: students design a pattern with the last 4 items blank, for a family member to complete.

---

### 5. Gallery Walk & Close *(10 min)*

Students lay their stamp strips on their desks. The class does a 2-minute silent gallery walk, looking at each other's work. Then: *"Which pattern had the most interesting core? Which was the hardest to extend?"*

**Bridge:** *"You have spotted patterns, extended them, and created your own. Next week we use everything — sequences, instructions, and patterns — to write your very first algorithm."*
